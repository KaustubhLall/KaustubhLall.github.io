What I built

  • ▸Defined a proposed fish-to-filter-to-grow loop with separate normal-flow, solids and emergency-drain paths.
  • ▸Turned open structural, electrical, hydraulic and stocking questions into an explicit review checklist instead of presenting them as solved.

The design brief

I am treating this as a compact systems-planning problem: keep water moving, separate solids before they reach the biological stage, make failure paths visible, and leave enough access to inspect and maintain the system. The public draft uses two planned tanks, tiered plant zones, two main racks and one auxiliary rack as design inputs, not as proof that equipment has been bought or installed.

The page is deliberately generalized. Exact balcony, wall, walkway, outlet, rack and tank measurements stay out of the public draft until they have been reviewed for privacy and feasibility. The diagrams explain relationships, while the planning ledger keeps candidate sizes and unresolved decisions visible.

System overview

The proposed loop sends water from the tank pair through solids-lifting overflows, a mechanical filter and a moving-bed biofilter into a sump. A DC return pump and manifold then send water to generalized plant zones before it returns to the tanks.

This is a communication diagram, not a hydraulic model. It does not establish turnover, pump head, drain capacity, biological maturity or structural adequacy. Those checks belong to the build review.

Water and solids flow

The planning brief separates normal water flow from captured solids and from an emergency dry-drain path. The proposed sequence is a shared header, radial-flow filter, moving-bed biofilter and sump, with a service point for solids handling rather than a hidden maintenance step.

The displayed pipe sizes are candidate planning inputs. Overflow sizing, receiving capacity, drain slope, pump failure behavior and the route to a safe discharge point remain unresolved.

Rack elevation concept

The rack drawing uses relative lower, middle and upper tiers so the public page can discuss plant placement and service access without publishing a precise home layout. Lower zones are intended for leafy herbs, while upper zones are reserved for larger herbs or bushes in the current concept.

Filled tanks, filters and wet media create a real structural and stability problem. Rack loading, floor loading, tip resistance, fasteners, wall conditions and access or egress require site-specific validation before construction.

Plumbing and return manifold

The proposed return manifold has six half-inch feeds for plant zones and two three-quarter-inch returns toward the tanks. Valves, unions, check valves and isolation points are shown as planning needs because a readable manifold is easier to test and service than a single opaque branch.

The count and candidate sizes are not a hydraulic design. Final pipe sizing, head loss, flow balance, endpoint elevations and pump selection must be checked together, including what happens when one branch is closed or a pump stops.

Safety and maintenance plan

Safety planning includes GFCI protection, drip-loop review, leak sensors, battery-backed aeration, a spare pump and visible dry-drain paths. The intent is to make loss of power, loss of return flow and a leak observable and recoverable rather than relying on one component to work forever.

The current air target is roughly 40 to 60 liters per minute, with battery runtime still to be determined. Electrical protection, enclosure placement, water exposure, emergency shutoff access and maintenance procedures remain open review items.

What the plan does not decide

The candidate livestock list includes kuhli loaches, pygmy Corydoras, bristlenose pleco, Neocaridina, dwarf crayfish and mystery snails because those animals came up in planning conversations. The list is not a compatibility result. Temperature, adult size, behavior, bioload, plant damage, water chemistry, disease control and welfare need a separate review before any stocking decision.

The useful outcome of this draft is a bounded set of questions: can the support structure carry the filled system, can normal and emergency paths handle credible flow, can wet electrical equipment be protected, can every filter and shutoff be reached, and can the planned livestock coexist? Until those answers are supported, the system remains a proposal.

proposed sequence

Build stages

  1. 01

    Review the boundary

    Keep the public page generalized, then check private measurements, rack loading, floor loading, tip resistance, access and egress locally.

  2. 02

    Bench-check the loop

    Confirm overflow behavior, dry-drain routing, filtration order, pump head and branch balance before committing to a full rack layout.

  3. 03

    Prototype one path

    Test one tank, one overflow path, filtration and a return branch with leak detection and a controlled shutdown plan.

  4. 04

    Add redundancy

    Check GFCI placement, drip loops, air-backup runtime, leak alerts, isolation valves and spare-pump changeover.

  5. 05

    Cycle and measure

    Verify water chemistry, temperature, flow, leak behavior and maintenance access before any stocking decision.

  6. 06

    Stage planting and livestock

    Choose plants and livestock only after compatibility, load and seasonal conditions are reviewed. The current species list stays provisional.

review gates

Questions still open

  • Rack and floor load: Can the structure and supporting surface carry the actual filled-system load with margin?
  • Tip resistance: Is each rack restrained, stable and serviceable without creating a new egress problem?
  • Access and egress: Can filters, valves, drains and electrical shutoffs be reached without blocking the walkway?
  • Overflow sizing: Can normal and emergency paths pass the worst credible flow without relying on a single pump or drain?
  • Electrical protection: Are GFCI, drip loops, enclosures, battery backup and shutoff procedures appropriate for the wet environment?
  • Stocking compatibility: Do temperature, behavior, bioload, plant needs and disease-control practices support any proposed combination?

This page is a planning and communication artifact. It does not certify structural adequacy, floor capacity, electrical safety, hydraulic performance, water quality or animal welfare.

notes

  • This is a proposed system, not a verified installation. Rack loading, floor loading, tip resistance, access and egress, overflow sizing, electrical protection and livestock compatibility still require review.
  • The public page intentionally omits exact home-layout measurements and personal location details. It uses generalized diagrams for discussion.
  • Candidate livestock are examples from planning conversations, not a validated stocking list or compatibility recommendation.
